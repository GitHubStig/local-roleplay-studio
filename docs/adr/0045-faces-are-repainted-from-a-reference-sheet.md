# Faces are repainted from a reference sheet, in a pass after each render and edit

An Image Model draws a person it doesn't know by name a little differently in every picture: Kael was a different man in each Frame, however the Look described him. Since 2026-11-26 a Storyboard or a Roleplay can be started with **reference sheets**: a render of each Subject from the front, at a three-quarter turn and in profile, made once from the Look before the story begins; and after each picture is rendered or edited, a **face pass** repaints each face it shows from that Subject's sheet. Built in stages, each recorded in [research](../research/people-in-pictures.md#reference-sheets-as-built-2026-11-26), on benches of both machines: [2026-11-22](../bench/reference-portraits/2026-11-22/README.md), [2026-11-25](../bench/reference-portraits/2026-11-25/README.md), [stage 0](../bench/reference-sheets/2026-11-25/README.md), [stage 4](../bench/reference-sheets/2026-11-26/README.md), [stage 5](../bench/reference-sheets/2026-11-26/edits/README.md), [stage 6](../bench/reference-sheets/2026-11-26/check/README.md).

- **Two passes, not one.** The usual way to use a reference picture is to pass it into the render (one pass). Today's models then pull the expression, the pose and any clothes the prompt doesn't name towards the reference's: Elara stopped crying, Kael turned out of his profile, both wore the portrait's black (2026-11-22). So each Frame renders from its Image Prompt alone, by any Image Model, and a face edit after it repaints only the heads, keeping the moment. It's about 3× a render (on the PC ~63 s for one person after an 18 s render). One pass stays possible behind the same interface (`FaceWay`, `server/faces/facePass.ts`), as a workaround for today's models: bench it again when an edit model is added or upgraded.
- **A sheet, not a portrait.** A single portrait's pose turned faces towards it (3 of 4 edits); a sheet of three views didn't (2026-11-25). It's one render by any Image Model, in the Look's style; a sheet from another model or style still works, as the edit repaints the face in the picture's own paint.
- **One view of the sheet, nearest the head's turn**, mirrored for a head facing right. The sheet whole left a face that barely moves as rendered (Elara, on every seed), and on a close-up could paste the three heads in; one view gave her the sheet's face every time, and can't paste three heads (stage 4).
- **The heads found by the Qwen3-VL in Qwen-Image 2.1**, by name and identity, with which way each is turned: the only finder of five that found every head and says whose it is (stage 0), and already loaded with the edit model. So the first version needs Qwen-Image 2.1 Edit, on mflux or a ComfyUI.
- **An edit per person**, the head's box grown by a fifth and softened, **started part-way from the picture** (strength 0.96 for Qwen-Image 2.1 Edit): one edit for two people lost a scowl and a turn; from noise a head in profile turned to the viewer, and some grew (stage 0b, stage 4). The strength is the edit model's own, measured, to bench again when it changes.
- **The instruction names what to keep**: the Frame's expression sentence, which the Frame keeps apart for it. "The same expression" lost Elara's tears and the troll's laugh (stage 0); the pass paints out whatever it isn't told to keep.
- **After an edit, a pass of its own**, in the same job, never one edit with both the player's instruction and the sheet (that brings back one pass's pull). Only the heads the painted area touches are repainted, told to keep what the edit changed (the player's instruction named): told the Frame's expression, it put back a smile and painted out a cap and a cut on every seed (stage 5). An edit of the whole picture repaints every head: a camera move (the Edit dialog's Camera list wrote it, `EditOrder.camera`) told the Frame's expression as a render's pass is, any other ("make him smile", nothing painted) told its change, as a painted area's is (since 2026-11-26; the mark is in `queue.json` v2).
- **Each pass is part of its version** (ADR 0021): what the picture was before it is kept beside it, and **Without the face pass** swaps the two. Each face edit is seeded one on from what it's made from (ADR 0011), and an edit made from the faces one on from the pass's last.
- **A sheet is checked before it's used**, by the same Qwen3-VL, three questions at once: the Subject shown three times, a person's or creature's from the front, at a three-quarter turn and in profile in that order (the face pass picks a view by its place), and no writing. FIBO 2.0 Turbo wrote names on sheets, Anima Turbo drew views out of order; the face pass's own question couldn't count a sheet's views. A sheet that fails is made again once at the next seed; one that fails again is kept and shown with why, for the player to Redo or Change…, as the player may want it anyway (stage 6).
- **Sheets are settled before the story begins**: made, redone, changed and ticked only in a Storyboard's plan review or a Roleplay's Cast review, so no picture is made from a sheet that later changes. Whether a Session has them is its own setting, from Settings as it starts (asked again in Start's menu), as its Image Model is (ADR 0015), and can be turned on or off in its Look until the story begins, so one made without them, or restarted, can have them (since 2026-11-26).

## Considered Options

- **One pass**, the sheets passed into the render: cheaper (~1.3× a render) but pulls expression, pose and clothes; and only an Image Model that takes references could do it.
- **Words alone.** What can be named (a hairstyle, a scar, a garment) holds best in the Look's words, which the render follows: the Princess's side buns held in all four Frames from the words alone, ~18 s a Frame. A sheet earns its cost for faces the model doesn't know by name (stage 4).
- **The whole sheet as the reference**, with a count of the heads after to catch a pasted sheet: the face that barely moves stayed as rendered; one view needs no count.
- **Other finders**: MediaPipe, RT-DETR, SAM 3.1, SDPose were faster but none says whose a head is, and some missed heads or boxed lamps (stage 0).
- **Checking the backdrop, or turning down a sheet with the style's lamp in it**: every backdrop was read as dark and plain, and whether a lamp harms the face pass is untried, so the check notes it only.
- **Other edit models**: FLUX.3 Klein's face edit barely moved the face, FIBO 2.0 Edit Turbo's drew it larger (2026-11-22); offered greyed, saying why.
- **A depth map for objects**: holds the shape at the sheet's angle, not the scene's; dropped (stage 0).

## Consequences

- `session.json` version 5 holds a Session's `sheets` setting, its `sheets` beside the Look (each with why its check found it unfit, `flaw`, when kept so), each written Frame's `expression`, and each picture's and edit's `faces` (the pass, and the version before it). `settings.json` version 3 has the Feature, `faceEditModel` and `sheetImageModel`.
- A picture of someone with a sheet takes about 3× as long (on the PC ~55 s for one person after an 18 s render, since its edit workflow keeps the references' attention on the GPU, 2026-11-26), on the Mac with mflux ~2.3× (~3.5 min for one person against ~1.5, since its Qwen-Image 2.1 Edit takes mflux's step cache, 2026-11-26; ~4× before), so it's the player's choice before a Session starts.
- The face pass paints out what the sheet hasn't and the instruction doesn't name: a render's bruises, a softened expression. Watch in play (open threads).
- What to keep in the Session manager can leave out the renders before the face pass; sheets are always kept, as every later pass needs them.
