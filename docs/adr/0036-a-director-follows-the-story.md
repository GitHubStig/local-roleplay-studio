# A Director follows the story

A Roleplay's music should follow the course of what's between its people, like a film's composer, not be picked for each Message: a conversation can turn from flirtatious to vulnerable without its place or subject changing, and most Messages should change nothing. So a Text Model call reads the story after each Reply, and music is planned as Cues over several Frames. Benched in [bench/music-director/](../bench/music-director/README.md).

- **The Director reads the whole story**, with thoughts, as the music can hear what isn't said, and the Cue playing with why it began: that's what lets it follow a course rather than react to each line. It held more of each story's Frames than reading only since the Cue began.
- **Director and cue writer are two calls**, for cleaner Cues and quicker Frames than one merged call, which was about even on its decisions.
- **Score the Roleplay reads with hindsight**, the course first and then only the turns, in chunks on a long story.
- **How often the music changes stays the Director's.** A rule holding a gentle change until a Cue had lasted a few Frames kept a fight's score playing over its aftermath, and a player can turn a story at any moment (the owner, 2026-11-09).
- **No setting for voices.** It's a choice most players can't judge up front, and it suits some moments and not others: the cue writer chooses, and a steer outranks it (the owner, 2026-11-09). A blind test showed voices do make a difference, faintly, on ACE-Step.

## Considered Options

- **An extractor feeding the Director a summary**: it would lose the details a course depends on.
- **A classifier model for emotions**: it labels a line, not the course of a relationship, and adds memory and swaps.
- **Stingers in live play**: music made in minutes lands after the moment.
- **The story borrowing from a song** (a Character quoting its lyrics): songs echo the story, never the other way round (the owner).
- **A percentage for intensity**: a model can't tell 35% from 40%, so Behaviour is three named steps, each a sentence it's given.
