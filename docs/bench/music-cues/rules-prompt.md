# The caption

Begin with the genre and the feeling; then the track's course, in order, from the first sound to the
last; then the room and the recording. A caption that names only a texture gets that texture unchanged
for the whole track.

- A reader moves on within seconds, so it begins with a tune, and what comes second arrives within the
  first few bars. The change the moment needs comes early, not near the end.
- It sits under a spoken scene: soft, slow, and warm in the low and middle of each instrument.
- Write the sound, never the story: the model hears subtext as its surface. Name no person, place or
  event.
- The model hears only this caption, so it stands alone: never mention the Cue before.
- Only say what should be there: naming a thing brings it in, even after "no". Leave out what the Score
  says to avoid, and the tempo, key and length.
- Keep the Score's genre, instruments and production. Each caption is one of a story's: it follows the
  Cue before, so it comes in only as strongly as the moment turns.
- Danger is a low pulse, stabs that break in and build, a near-silent break midway, then a surge back.
  Folk instruments can't carry it.
- Voices need naming in the caption, and lines of vowels to sing in the lyrics.
