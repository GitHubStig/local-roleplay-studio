- **Posing a Character with a ControlNet** (the owner's idea; first noted 2026-10-09 for mflux 0.22, as composition control). Prompts pose people badly: arms, hands, how someone sits. A ControlNet renders a picture to follow a control picture as well as its prompt; given a pose (an OpenPose skeleton, which a posed 3D figure can make), it directs the body and limbs and leaves the rest to the prompt. The idea: pose a figure (a small 3D mannequin in the app, or a pose picked from saved ones) and render the Frame to it. It pairs with "Reference portraits for Subject consistency" below, a portrait holding the face and the pose the body. What's there: `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-controlnet` runs Alibaba's Fun ControlNet Union on Qwen-Image 2.1 (one checkpoint for a pose, depth, canny, lineart and more; `--controlnet-strength`, 1.0 the strongest), and mflux 0.23 adds ControlNets for Z-Image (`mflux-generate-z-image-controlnet`) and Krea 2 (`mflux-generate-krea2-controlnet`). All mflux, so the Mac only. *The PC's route* (noted 2026-11-02, from its model card; not downloaded or tried): MOSS-Image's colour/depth control on ComfyUI (`darask0/MOSS-Image-color-depth-control`, Apache-2.0, a community adapter; Comfy-Org repackages it as `moss_image_color_depth_control.safetensors`, supported since ComfyUI 0.43.0), on an Image Model the PC already runs. It takes a depth map (near is white), a colour map (flat colours marking regions, one prompt per colour), or both, and is made for "posing scenes with 3D mannequins and for region-painted layouts": placement, scale and pose follow the map, the prompt the rest. The region prompts could also say where each of two people stands. Its card warns that pose is coarse ("fingers and face details are generated by the model"), that a mannequin's surface can show through unless control ends halfway (`color_end` and `depth_end` 0.5), and that it was checked on a handful of scenes. A first try needs no mannequin: a depth map made from an existing picture. Not downloaded or tried: size, peak memory, speed, and whether it loads from the saved 8-bit copy. The same ControlNet with a depth map is the second way in "The next Frame's picture made from this one", above.

- **Reference portraits for Subject consistency** (rethought 2026-10-06 for mflux 0.21; split from picture edits 2026-10-22). The people in a Roleplay or Storyboard drift between Frames (a new seed, a new face), and the Look's words can only hold them so far. The idea: a portrait of each person, made once from the Look, passed with every render ("image 1 is Kael, image 2 is Elara Vance", then the Image Prompt). Each picture is still composed from its prompt, so the story leads and ADR 0005 holds (the Image Prompt is the state); the portraits only hold faces, hair and build steady. mflux 0.21's `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-edit` runs Qwen-Image 2.1 with up to ten reference images, the references' attention cached across steps (`--use-kv-cache`, on by default); Viggle's turbo LoRA does it with 1–3 references in 6 steps. *Inputs must be RGB* (noted 2026-10-07): Qwen-Image 2.1's fourth channel is an edit mask, and a transparent input stays transparent, so a reference with alpha would come back see-through where it isn't opaque. ComfyUI's pictures were RGBA with alpha at 240–255 until 2026-10-07 (research/comfyui.md, "Alpha"), and older ones in Sessions still are: convert them to RGB first. The one deliberate exception is a cut-out on transparency (BiRefNet), e.g. a person alone as a reference. *Tried 2026-10-06* ([bench/reference-portraits](bench/reference-portraits/README.md)): portraits of Kael and Elara from the Kael tavern Roleplay's Look, then Frames 6, 9 and 12 without and with them. **The portraits hold identity well**: without them Elara is two different women in 9 and 12 and Kael's face changes in 12; with them both match their portraits in every Frame, and the two people stay apart. Costs: 1.4–1.7× the time (122–142 s against 84–86 s at 1024², Mac, some of it likely the edit tool's guidance), the same ~39 GB peak, the full 33 GB weights (the saved 8-bit copy can't take references), and the references pulling against the prompt (wider shots, smaller people; Frame 12 lost Elara's knives). Next: whether prompt wording or guidance fixes the composition, then portraits made once per Roleplay and passed with every render (Qwen-Image 2.1 only). Slots in behind `ImageGenerator`. *Another way, from mflux 0.23* (noted 2026-10-17): `mflux-generate-flux3-edit` runs FLUX.3 Klein (an Image Model since 2026-10-17) with up to ten reference images, in 16 steps, and takes `-q 8` (Qwen-Image 2.1's saved 8-bit copy can't take references). Smaller than Qwen-Image 2.1 (a 5.6B transformer, ~20 GB against 33 GB); mflux's README: an edit is described as a full caption of the result, not an instruction. Not tried: whether its portraits hold identity as well as Qwen-Image 2.1's did, or its speed with references. Nor whether ComfyUI (the PC) can pass references with either model. *When someone's looks change* (noted 2026-10-22): a portrait holds a face against the prompt, so after "I turn into a demon" it would pull the picture back towards the old face. But a Roleplay's pictures already keep the old looks, since the Look is copied word for word into every one ("Bringing a Roleplay's Look up to date with the story", below): portraits make that problem stronger, not new. So a portrait belongs to the Look: made again when the Look is edited (one render), and dropped from a Frame whose prompt changes that person's looks for a while (a disguise, a mask, a one-scene transformation). Re-rendering a Frame from before the change would get the new portrait, as it gets the new Look today. FIBO 2.0 Edit Turbo is another route for these and for idle variant Frames (noted 2026-10-27): 4 steps, up to 5 reference images on mflux and 16 on ComfyUI.

- **JSON captions from the Art Agent: Ideogram 4, FIBO 1.5 and 2.0, MOSS-Image** (noted 2026-10-11, mflux 0.22; the owner wants it explored soon, 2026-10-27, once FIBO 2.0 turned out to be trained on captions as well). Ideogram 4 is in Settings but needs structured JSON captions (a high-level description, a style, and elements with bounding boxes and colours; Ideogram's prompting guide): from a prose Image Prompt it stamps a line of garbled lettering across the picture, while the same scene as a caption came out clean (Mac, one test scene; research/image-models.md). A caption would be a second picturing format for the Art Agent (the tags style was one, removed 2026-10-26: ADR 0023), passed through `--prompt-file` (mflux takes a caption in `--prompt` too). FIBO 1.5 (added 2026-10-12) also wants JSON captions, and so does FIBO 2.0 (whose mflux README calls prose "freeform text that works less well"), but mflux writes them itself from prose with FIBO's own VLM (`--expand-prompt`, `briaai/FIBO-vlm`, 8.9 GB); the Art Agent writing them instead would save that model's load and keep one Text Model writing every prompt. Until then Ideogram 4 is only worth picking for a hand-written Image Prompt in caption form. MOSS-Image (an Image Model on ComfyUI since 2026-10-19) takes a layout caption too, built by ComfyUI's `MOSSImageLayoutPrompt` from boxes with captions and text to render, so one caption format might serve both. *ComfyUI's route* (checked 2026-10-27 on the PC, ComfyUI 0.43.0): the app's `fibo-2.0` workflow sends prose straight into `TextEncodeFibo2`, which takes plain text or a JSON caption, with no VLM; ComfyUI's own FIBO 2.0 edit templates (`image_fibo2_edit_dev_pe`) add an optional Prompt Enhancer, a `TextGenerate` node on Qwen3-VL-8B (`qwen3vl_8b_int8_convrot`) with Bria's rewrite prompt as its system prompt, so a caption could be made there without the Art Agent; its notes say a structured rewrite kept an edit to the one requested change where prose with "keep X unchanged" invented props. *Tool to build first* (noted 2026-10-27): a small script in `docs/bench/` that runs `FiboVLM(quantize=…).generate(prompt=…, seed=42)` alone (mflux's own interpreter, `~/.local/share/uv/tools/mflux/bin/python`; there's no mflux command for prompt to caption, only `mflux-inspire-fibo` for an image and `mflux-refine-fibo` for an existing caption), printing the caption and how long it took, so a hand-written caption can be compared with the VLM's and the VLM's share of a render timed (`--expand-prompt` loads the 8.9 GB VLM, writes up to 4096 tokens, frees it, then loads FIBO). Also unchecked: Ming-Image (which reads prose well) over many Frames, and keeping a Subject consistent with either. *mflux's caption path* (FIBO 2.0, 2026-10-27, Mac): the same caption came out photographic on mflux where on ComfyUI, and prose on both, it gave a painting; mflux drops empty fields and scores and compacts the caption before encoding it (unchecked whether that's why), so check it before the Art Agent's captions go to FIBO on mflux. *Also blocked* (2026-10-14, Mac, mflux 0.22): a plain tavern prompt (lamps, a bar, rain on a window) came back as a black "Image blocked by safety filter" picture at seed 42, 512², on both the 12- and 20-step presets; mflux warns that plain prompts "increase safety-filter false positives" (`ideogram4_text_encoder/caption.py`).

- **TypeScript 7 for the web app** (noted 2026-10-21, Mac; parked the same day). The web is type-checked by vue-tsc 3.3, which patches TypeScript's JavaScript compiler; TypeScript 7.0.2 (the Go rewrite) no longer ships it (`lib/tsc`), so vue-tsc fails at start (vuejs/language-tools #6124 and #6156, closed in favour of #5381), and Microsoft's TypeScript 7 announcement says Vue workflows can't use 7 yet, for want of a stable programmatic API. Two ways on: [typescript-native-bridge](https://github.com/johnsoncodehk/typescript-native-bridge), the Vue tools maintainer's fork of the `typescript` package with tsgo underneath, swapped in by a package override (the Vue tools repo checks itself with it, ~2.7× faster; untried here, nor whether Deno's npm support takes the override); or TypeScript 7.1's content mappers, which let `tsc` check `.vue` files itself (microsoft/typescript-go #4712, merged 2026-08-19). 7.1 is in beta (2026-10-20, content mappers and a new programmatic API included, both still being refined), with a release candidate due in about a month and the final release shortly after. Vue's side is Vue Language Tools v4 (vuejs/language-tools #6170, an open alpha on 2026-10-21): `tsc --runExternalCode` with `@vue/content-mapper` under `contentMappers` in `tsconfig.json` replaces `vue-tsc`, and that package isn't on npm yet. Here the whole check takes ~2 s, so the bridge would save about a second for a third-party fork: not worth it. Trigger: TypeScript 7.1 final and a released Vue Language Tools v4; then move the web's check to `tsc` with the content mapper and drop vue-tsc. The server's `deno check` is Deno's own TypeScript and isn't affected.
