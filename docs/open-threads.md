- **Reference images and edits for Subject consistency** (rethought 2026-10-06 for mflux 0.21). The people in a Roleplay or Storyboard drift between Frames (a new seed, a new face), and the Look's words can only hold them so far. mflux 0.21's `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-edit` runs Qwen-Image 2.1 (the model already rendered with, its saved 8-bit copy too) with up to ten reference images, instruction edits with a strength, region repaints from a mask or a description (`--auto-mask "the red shirt"`, found by its Qwen3-VL), and the references' attention cached across steps (`--use-kv-cache`, on by default); Viggle's turbo LoRA does edits with 1–3 references in 6 steps. *Inputs must be RGB* (noted 2026-10-07): Qwen-Image 2.1's fourth channel is an edit mask, and a transparent input stays transparent, so a reference or an edited picture with alpha would come back see-through where it isn't opaque. ComfyUI's pictures were RGBA with alpha at 240–255 until 2026-10-07 (research/comfyui.md, "Alpha"), and older ones in Sessions still are: convert them to RGB first. The one deliberate exception is a cut-out on transparency (BiRefNet), e.g. a person alone as a reference. Three ways to use it:
  1. *Reference portraits (Roleplay, Storyboard; the most promising).* A portrait of each person, made once from the Look, passed with every render ("image 1 is Kael, image 2 is Elara Vance", then the Image Prompt). Each picture is still composed from its prompt, so the story leads and ADR 0005 holds (the Image Prompt is the state); the portraits only hold faces, hair and build steady.
  2. *Editing the previous picture (Chain).* An Action as an edit instruction, so what it doesn't mention stays exactly as it was. But the picture then carries what the edits left, not what its prompt says (against ADR 0005), flaws build up over many edits, and some Actions (a new art style) are better re-rendered. A middle path: edit only Actions that change one thing.
  3. *Fixing one part (any kind).* Describe what's wrong ("her left hand") and repaint just that region.
  *Tried 2026-10-06* ([bench/reference-portraits](bench/reference-portraits/README.md)): portraits of Kael and Elara from the Kael tavern Roleplay's Look, then Frames 6, 9 and 12 without and with them. **The portraits hold identity well**: without them Elara is two different women in 9 and 12 and Kael's face changes in 12; with them both match their portraits in every Frame, and the two people stay apart. Costs: 1.4–1.7× the time (122–142 s against 84–86 s at 1024², some of it likely the edit tool's guidance), the same ~39 GB peak, the full 33 GB weights (the saved 8-bit copy can't take references), and the references pulling against the prompt (wider shots, smaller people; Frame 12 lost Elara's knives). Next: whether prompt wording or guidance fixes the composition, then portraits made once per Roleplay and passed with every render (Qwen-Image 2.1 only). Slots in behind `ImageGenerator`.
  *Another way, from mflux 0.23* (noted 2026-10-17): `mflux-generate-flux3-edit` runs FLUX.3 Klein (an Image Model since 2026-10-17) with up to ten reference images, in 16 steps, and takes `-q 8` (Qwen-Image 2.1's saved 8-bit copy can't take references). Smaller than Qwen-Image 2.1 (a 5.6B transformer, ~20 GB against 33 GB); mflux's README: an edit is described as a full caption of the result, not an instruction. Not tried: whether its portraits hold identity as well as Qwen-Image 2.1's did.
- **Plan, review, then Write: a Storyboard written from a reviewed plan** (noted 2026-10-16; replaces "A rename that carries into the text" and "Adding someone to several Frames at once"). A Storyboard's plan casts its Brief badly at times (someone missing from the Look, or there with no name, only "demon"), and a fix by hand afterwards doesn't reach the Frames' sentences: they keep the old name, or leave the newcomer out. So the plan would come in two steps:
  1. *Plan*: one call writes the Look and the Beats, and who each Beat shows. The Storyboard stops there for review: each Subject's name and identity, the art style, each Beat's line and its Shows toggles, all editable; nothing is written out or rendered yet.
  2. *Write Frames*: a button that writes each Frame's seven sentences from the reviewed Look and Beats, a call per Frame (below), the Beats checked with the term list first, as an Action is.
  After Write, the Look and Beats are fixed: no renames, new Subjects or style changes. An Action edits only its own Frame (its sentences and Shows, from the Subjects already in the Look) and declines what would change the Look. **Restart**, with a confirm, takes the Storyboard back to its plan, with its Look and Beats as they were and every Frame's sentences and pictures gone. Neither a rename's find-and-replace nor "Add to Frames…" is then needed, and part of ADR 0006 (one call for the whole plan) is replaced: an ADR when it's built.
  - *The edit request drops the Beats* (Mac, 2026-10-16, Gemma 4 26B Heretic nvfp4, Thinking off). An Action is sent with every Frame's Beat now. 72 edits of two tavern Frames, six Actions (vague, leaning on the story, a control), with every Beat, only the Frame's own, or none: every one done, none unclear, and alike each way. The other Beats didn't help continuity: "bring back the letter" put it in the traveller's hand or on the floor with them, though the Beats have Kael holding it. A Beat stays the plan's line; an edit doesn't update it.
  - *Write a call per Frame* (Mac, 2026-10-16, the same model). Each call has the Frames before it, as one conversation with every Beat given first. Against one call: 1.2× the time at 8 Frames (about 26 s against 22), 1.5× at 32 (111 s against 74). One call of 32 Frames slipped two Frames behind its Beats from Frame 19 on, so their people were no longer who each Frame shows, and grew a third terser by its last quarter (76 to 49 words a Frame); a call per Frame can't slip, and kept 100–113 words. Seeing the Beats ahead made no difference: two late payoffs (a pistol hidden under the bar, a letter left on it) were set up in no earlier Frame either way. Ollama's MLX engine doesn't reuse a shared start of the prompt (the same 2,075 tokens read in 1.27 s, then with five more tokens on the end in 1.04 s), but reading is fast enough that the growing conversation added only half a second a call by Frame 32.
  - *Today's 32-Frame plan slips too*: in the same bench the plan call ran about one Frame late by its end (Frames 28 and 32 checked), as `shown` once did in a 32-Frame plan (research/people-in-pictures.md). It stays so until Write is built.
  - *Casting* (Mac, 2026-10-16). Plans of five Briefs with unnamed people, a demon, a dog and two vehicles. Thinking didn't help, at 5–8× the time (150–325 s a plan): 7 of 14 unnamed people named with it off and 8 with it on (26B), 8 and 5 (Gemma 4 12B Heretic nvfp4); and a demon in the Beats was left out of the Look in three of the four ways on one seed. "The traveller" and "the demon" stayed unnamed, even with a sentence saying that's not a name; the review is where they get one. `shared/identity.md` is the Roleplay Art Agent's too, so the new wording would reach Roleplays: check one there.

- **Qwen-Image 2.1 ControlNet for composition** (noted 2026-10-09, mflux 0.22). `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-controlnet` runs Alibaba's Fun ControlNet Union on Qwen-Image 2.1: a control picture at the target framing (canny, depth, grayscale, HED, lineart, MLSD, pose or scribble; one checkpoint reads all of them, no type flag) and `--controlnet-strength` (1.0 the strongest, 0 off), plus inpainting with `--image-path` and `--mask-image` (white is repainted from the prompt, black kept). mflux takes the control picture ready-made, so the app would have to make it (a depth map from Depth Pro, which mflux ships for `mflux-save-depth`; a pose from a pose model). Uses: keeping a Frame's staging when only the Subject or the light should change (the previous Frame's depth map as the control), steadying composition where reference portraits pull shots wider (above), and a pose the player sketches. Not downloaded or tried; unknown: its size and peak memory, whether it loads from the saved 8-bit copy, and its speed against a plain render.
- **Upscales in the model comparison export** (noted 2026-10-14). The exported page's thumbnails come from each original picture, but its full-size view takes the upscale where there is one (`server/export/comparison.ts`, `p.upscaled ?? p.image`; the Compare dialog does the same). If only some models' pictures were upscaled, those look sharper full size, which flatters them in a comparison of the models themselves. Options: always export the originals, or a switch in the export dialog (originals, or upscales where there are any).
- **Idle animation between Frames.** Keep the image alive while the player is thinking: flickering candles, drifting haze, the Subject blinking or shifting her weight, with the Image Prompt itself unchanged. Idle work must never block a Frame: it runs only after a commit, and is cancelled the moment the player sends an Action, since it competes for the same GPU and memory. Options, cheapest first:
  1. *Browser-only effects.* A slow pan and zoom over the still, plus ambient overlays picked from Image Prompt keywords (haze gives drifting fog, candles give a warm flicker, a rim light gives a soft pulse). Instant, and no extra rendering. Effects can't know *where* the candles are, so they stay generic.
  2. *Subject mask plus layered effects.* Cut the Subject out of the finished image with a background-removal model (mflux lists `fibo-edit-rmbg`; not downloaded yet, unverified), then animate only the background layer (fog, flicker, slight parallax) behind her. Better than rendering the background and Subject separately and compositing: separate renders don't share lighting, shadows or perspective, so the Subject looks pasted on.
  3. *Idle variant frames via image editing.* After commit, edit the Frame's image into a few near-identical frames ("eyes closed", "weight on the other foot", "candle flames taller") and crossfade or flash between them; a 150 ms eyes-closed frame every few seconds reads as a blink. Instruction-based editors in mflux 0.20: `mflux-generate-flux2-edit` (FLUX.2 Klein; weights already cached, likely ~15 s per frame), `mflux-generate-qwen-edit` (Qwen Image Edit, ~20B, not downloaded, much slower), `mflux-generate-kontext`, and `mflux-generate-fibo-edit`, which takes a `--mask-path` to edit only the background. The engine, not the player, writes these edit instructions, from a fixed list, so they stay within the Shoot Brief. *Revisited 2026-10-06 for mflux 0.21:* `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-edit --auto-mask "his eyes"` finds a region from a description (its Qwen3-VL) and repaints only that ("his eyes closed"); pasting just that region back onto the original through a feathered mask keeps every other pixel exact (a whole-picture edit re-encodes it, and the swap would shimmer). Good for brief swaps (a blink, a flicker, a glance); not for motion (two poses crossfaded double-expose, so options 1 and 2 stay better for that). Each variant is a full edit render (unmeasured, likely a minute or more at 1024²) on the full Qwen-Image 2.1 weights (33 GB; the saved 8-bit copy can't edit), and renders unload the Text Model first, so variants made while the player types would make the next Reply reload it: make them after a pause, or when the picture viewer opens. *Tried 2026-10-06* ([bench/reference-portraits](bench/reference-portraits/README.md)): the composite works (99.75% of pixels byte-identical, only a box around the eyes touched), but the blink didn't (Elara's eyes stayed open; 0.01% of pixels moved). Retried the same day with demos ([bench/idle-animation](bench/idle-animation/README.md), in 2D and in SHARP's 3D): a hand-drawn eye mask and "eyelids fully closed" (not "blink") work; shown half, closed, half, the blink looks natural, and so does a crossfade, but a flashed "closed" alone is cartoony. The owner's picks: a calm flat glow on the lamps by default; **the painted light (an edit of the lamp flaring, faded in and out) is the standout**; a 2D flame wobble; depth glow too much indoors; painted flame shapes too much. All of it held up in 3D (glow as a light in the room, the edits as colour fades on the same splats), apart from the wobble there. For the app, the open problems are finding the lamps, flames and eyes reliably (the auto-mask and a brightness search both miss), and the edits' cost: ~2 min per lamp, ~4 min per person's blink.
  4. *True image-to-video* (a 2–4 s loop from the still). mflux has no video models; this needs a separate local runtime and likely takes minutes per clip on a Mac. Out of reach for now.

- **JSON captions for Ideogram 4** (noted 2026-10-11, mflux 0.22). Ideogram 4 is in Settings but needs structured JSON captions (a high-level description, a style, and elements with bounding boxes and colours; Ideogram's prompting guide): from a prose Image Prompt it stamps a line of garbled lettering across the picture, while the same scene as a caption came out clean (Mac, one test scene; models.md). A caption would be a second picturing format for the Art Agent, like the tags it already offers (`art-frame-tags.md`), passed through `--prompt-file` (mflux takes a caption in `--prompt` too). FIBO 1.5 (added 2026-10-12) also wants JSON captions, but mflux writes them itself from prose with FIBO's own VLM; the Art Agent writing them instead would save that model's load and keep one Text Model writing every prompt. Until then Ideogram 4 is only worth picking for a hand-written Image Prompt in caption form. Also unchecked: Ming-Image (which reads prose well) over many Frames, and keeping a Subject consistent with either. *Also blocked* (2026-10-14, Mac, mflux 0.22): a plain tavern prompt (lamps, a bar, rain on a window) came back as a black "Image blocked by safety filter" picture at seed 42, 512², on both the 12- and 20-step presets; mflux warns that plain prompts "increase safety-filter false positives" (`ideogram4_text_encoder/caption.py`).
- **Viggle's 9-step mode for Qwen-Image 2.1.** The Image Model Qwen-Image 2.1 Turbo (Viggle LoRA) runs Viggle's turbo LoRA at 6 steps (v0.3, `docs/models.md`). Viggle's v0.3 also has a 9-step mode, with the same LoRA: 7 turbo steps, then the LoRA is switched off and the base model takes the last two (its own sigmas, `[1.0, 0.9583, 0.9167, 0.875, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 1/6, 1/12]`; the prompt's cached K/V are computed again on the first base step). Finer detail and small text right more often, at ~1.4–1.5× the time of 6 steps; Viggle says it works in diffusers and its Space only. mflux 0.21's `viggle_turbo` scheduler requires exactly 6 steps and can't switch a LoRA off partway (checked 2026-10-04). Doing it ourselves means a Python script on mflux's internals (a custom scheduler, a hook after step 7), which any mflux update could break. Trigger: mflux supports it, or 6 steps looks too soft.
- **Qwen-Image 2.1 Turbo's text encoder stored twice** (noted 2026-10-17, Mac, mflux 0.23). Turbo's repo holds Qwen-Image 2.1's text encoder byte for byte (all 750 tensors), split into one file instead of four, so the cache keeps 17.5 GB of it twice (models.md). Linking Turbo's `text_encoder/` to the base model's files would free it, but it's a hand edit inside the Hugging Face cache that an mflux upgrade could undo; the owner chose to keep both for now. Check with each mflux release: whether mflux can load a text encoder from another repo, or Qwen re-shards one to match the other. Or drop Turbo, if Viggle's LoRA (about as fast, 1.3 GB) serves as well.
- **A figure inside its 2.5D scene: SHARP's background with a TripoSplat or LiTo figure.** Tried 2026-10-04 (docs/research/image-to-3d.md, "a figure inside its 2.5D scene"): works, and makes a figure look far better than alone on black, though the person becomes the figure's redrawing at the picture's own view. Method: remove the splats SHARP put inside the person's cut-out mask (and not far behind it), turn the figure to face the camera, scale and move it to cover the mask from the picture's camera (outline fit 0.90–0.95), append it in SHARP's axes. To build into the app: when a Frame has a scene and a figure, view them together (a "with the room" choice on the figure's viewer, or a combined file made after both), orbiting as the figure viewer does but with the room only holding to ~30°; and decide what the picture's view should show (the figure, or SHARP's person until it turns).
