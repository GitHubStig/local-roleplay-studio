# Open threads

Ideas deliberately deferred. Promote an item to an ADR in `docs/adr/` when we act on it.

- **Persistent image sidecar.** Replace per-Frame `mflux-generate-*` CLI calls with a small Python service that keeps the Image Model loaded and exposes `POST /generate`. Measured 2026-09-24, loading was ~5 s of a 42 s Z-Image Turbo render, so it was parked. Measured again 2026-10-05, it matters for the fast paths: Qwen-Image 2.1 in Fast mode (6 steps, 512², 8-bit) denoised in 5 s of a 32.7 s run, so ~27 s of every Fast render is starting up (loading ~22 GB, plus encoding the prompt and decoding the picture; the split not measured, loading surely the most); Upscale (SeedVR2) is ~46 s, mostly loading; FLUX.2 Klein 4B is ~4–5 s in all at 512². A loaded model could bring a Fast render from ~30 s to well under 10 s. Against it, memory: a loaded Qwen-Image holds 20–39 GB, and renders now unload Ollama first because two big models don't fit on 48 GB, so the sidecar would have to unload after a short idle (as the voice service does after a minute) and be unloaded before the Text Model runs (mirroring `freeMemory`). It pays off for back-to-back renders (Render all, a few Fast renders in a row), not a Roleplay where renders alternate with Replies and it would reload each time. A ComfyUI backend (below) is this already, a server that keeps models loaded: if that's built, this folds into it. Trigger: waiting on Fast or Render all. Slots in behind `ImageGenerator`.
- **Reference images and edits for Subject consistency** (rethought 2026-10-06 for mflux 0.21). The people in a Roleplay or Storyboard drift between Frames (a new seed, a new face), and the Look's words can only hold them so far. mflux 0.21's `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-edit` runs Qwen-Image 2.1 (the model already rendered with, its saved 8-bit copy too) with up to ten reference images, instruction edits with a strength, region repaints from a mask or a description (`--auto-mask "the red shirt"`, found by its Qwen3-VL), and the references' attention cached across steps (`--use-kv-cache`, on by default); Viggle's turbo LoRA does edits with 1–3 references in 6 steps. Three ways to use it:
  1. *Reference portraits (Roleplay, Storyboard; the most promising).* A portrait of each person, made once from the Look, passed with every render ("image 1 is Kael, image 2 is Elara Vance", then the Image Prompt). Each picture is still composed from its prompt, so the story leads and ADR 0005 holds (the Image Prompt is the state); the portraits only hold faces, hair and build steady.
  2. *Editing the previous picture (Chain).* An Action as an edit instruction, so what it doesn't mention stays exactly as it was. But the picture then carries what the edits left, not what its prompt says (against ADR 0005), flaws build up over many edits, and some Actions (a new art style) are better re-rendered. A middle path: edit only Actions that change one thing.
  3. *Fixing one part (any kind).* Describe what's wrong ("her left hand") and repaint just that region.
  *Tried 2026-10-06* ([bench/reference-portraits](bench/reference-portraits/README.md)): portraits of Kael and Elara from the Kael tavern Roleplay's Look, then Frames 6, 9 and 12 without and with them. **The portraits hold identity well**: without them Elara is two different women in 9 and 12 and Kael's face changes in 12; with them both match their portraits in every Frame, and the two people stay apart. Costs: 1.4–1.7× the time (122–142 s against 84–86 s at 1024², some of it likely the edit tool's guidance), the same ~39 GB peak, the full 33 GB weights (the saved 8-bit copy can't take references), and the references pulling against the prompt (wider shots, smaller people; Frame 12 lost Elara's knives). Next: whether prompt wording or guidance fixes the composition, then portraits made once per Roleplay and passed with every render (Qwen-Image 2.1 only). Slots in behind `ImageGenerator`.

- **Narration that doesn't match the Image Prompt.** The Narration is the Text Model's own list of what changed, so a small model can list a change it didn't make, or miss one it did. The Prompt tab's word diff comes from comparing the paragraphs, so it's always right. Seen with spark-x2.5 4B: the Narration claimed "Expression: scared" while the prompt still said she was smiling. - **Idle animation between Frames.** Keep the image alive while the player is thinking: flickering candles, drifting haze, the Subject blinking or shifting her weight, with the Image Prompt itself unchanged. Idle work must never block a Frame: it runs only after a commit, and is cancelled the moment the player sends an Action, since it competes for the same GPU and memory. Options, cheapest first:
  1. *Browser-only effects.* A slow pan and zoom over the still, plus ambient overlays picked from Image Prompt keywords (haze gives drifting fog, candles give a warm flicker, a rim light gives a soft pulse). Instant, and no extra rendering. Effects can't know *where* the candles are, so they stay generic.
  2. *Subject mask plus layered effects.* Cut the Subject out of the finished image with a background-removal model (mflux lists `fibo-edit-rmbg`; not downloaded yet, unverified), then animate only the background layer (fog, flicker, slight parallax) behind her. Better than rendering the background and Subject separately and compositing: separate renders don't share lighting, shadows or perspective, so the Subject looks pasted on.
  3. *Idle variant frames via image editing.* After commit, edit the Frame's image into a few near-identical frames ("eyes closed", "weight on the other foot", "candle flames taller") and crossfade or flash between them; a 150 ms eyes-closed frame every few seconds reads as a blink. Instruction-based editors in mflux 0.20: `mflux-generate-flux2-edit` (FLUX.2 Klein; weights already cached, likely ~15 s per frame), `mflux-generate-qwen-edit` (Qwen Image Edit, ~20B, not downloaded, much slower), `mflux-generate-kontext`, and `mflux-generate-fibo-edit`, which takes a `--mask-path` to edit only the background. The engine, not the player, writes these edit instructions, from a fixed list, so they stay within the Shoot Brief. *Revisited 2026-10-06 for mflux 0.21:* `mflux-generate-qwen-2.1-edit --auto-mask "his eyes"` finds a region from a description (its Qwen3-VL) and repaints only that ("his eyes closed"); pasting just that region back onto the original through a feathered mask keeps every other pixel exact (a whole-picture edit re-encodes it, and the swap would shimmer). Good for brief swaps (a blink, a flicker, a glance); not for motion (two poses crossfaded double-expose, so options 1 and 2 stay better for that). Each variant is a full edit render (unmeasured, likely a minute or more at 1024²) on the full Qwen-Image 2.1 weights (33 GB; the saved 8-bit copy can't edit), and renders unload the Text Model first, so variants made while the player types would make the next Reply reload it: make them after a pause, or when the picture viewer opens. *Tried 2026-10-06* ([bench/reference-portraits](bench/reference-portraits/README.md)): the composite works (99.75% of pixels byte-identical, only a box around the eyes touched), but the blink didn't (Elara's eyes stayed open; 0.01% of pixels moved). Retried the same day with demos ([bench/idle-animation](bench/idle-animation/README.md), in 2D and in SHARP's 3D): a hand-drawn eye mask and "eyelids fully closed" (not "blink") work; shown half, closed, half, the blink looks natural, and so does a crossfade, but a flashed "closed" alone is cartoony. The owner's picks: a calm flat glow on the lamps by default; **the painted light (an edit of the lamp flaring, faded in and out) is the standout**; a 2D flame wobble; depth glow too much indoors; painted flame shapes too much. All of it held up in 3D (glow as a light in the room, the edits as colour fades on the same splats), apart from the wobble there. For the app, the open problems are finding the lamps, flames and eyes reliably (the auto-mask and a brightness search both miss), and the edits' cost: ~2 min per lamp, ~4 min per person's blink.
  4. *True image-to-video* (a 2–4 s loop from the still). mflux has no video models; this needs a separate local runtime and likely takes minutes per clip on a Mac. Out of reach for now.

- **Windows, and backends other than Ollama and mflux: the plan** (2026-10-06). The aim: the app runs on Windows (or any machine) with only Ollama installed, and each extra (images, voices, 3D) is on when its backend is there and off, hidden, when it isn't. Checked against the code on 2026-10-06, nothing run on Windows yet:
  - *What breaks on Windows today, with only Ollama.* The server, the web app and Ollama run; a Roleplay's conversation, Suggest and a Storyboard's plan work. **A Chain can't start**: its Opening Frame must render, and the default image backend is mflux (Mac only), so only `IMAGE_GENERATOR=placeholder` gets past it. Render and Upscale fail ("mflux-generate-… not found"); voices fail (mlx-audio, Mac only); SHARP and TripoSplat fail (their scripts use the Mac GPU, `mps`); LiTo fails (mlx-spatial, Mac only). Two plain bugs too: the first child process (any render, voice or 3D) throws, because `children.ts` listens for `SIGTERM` and Deno on Windows allows only `SIGINT` and `SIGBREAK`; and the uv scripts are passed as `new URL(…).pathname`, which is `/C:/…` on Windows (`fromFileUrl` is right).
  - *Order:*
    1. *(Done 2026-10-06; to confirm on Windows.)* **Run on Windows without crashing:** signals by platform, `fromFileUrl` for the scripts, and each Python script picking `cuda`, `mps` or `cpu`, and on Windows and Linux fetching torch from PyTorch's CUDA index (PyPI's is CPU-only there; `uv tree --script … --python-platform x86_64-pc-windows-msvc` resolves `torch 2.11.0+cu128`). Small; worth it whatever follows.
    2. *(Done 2026-10-06, `server/features.ts`; to confirm on Windows.)* **What's available, decided at startup; Settings in tabs.** The server checks the platform, mflux on the PATH, uv, and the GPU, and reports each feature (images, voices, SHARP, TripoSplat, LiTo) as available or not, with why (`/api/settings/options` or `/api/health`). An unavailable feature is off and its buttons hidden (made audio, scenes and figures still play and open); available ones can be switched off in Settings. This replaces `VOICES`, `SCENES`, `FIGURES` and `LITO=off`. Settings grows tabs: **Text** (model, Thinking, Art Agent, Limits), **Images** (backend, model, size, steps, and mflux's Quantize, Step cache and Fast), **Voice**, **3D**. About two days.
    3. **An OpenAI-compatible Text backend** beside Ollama, for LM Studio, llama.cpp's server, and cloud services (OpenAI, OpenRouter): `/v1/chat/completions` with `response_format: json_schema`, `/v1/models`, a base URL and an API key in Settings. Today the text goes through Ollama's own API for its JSON-schema `format`, `think`, `/api/tags` and `/api/show`, and `keep_alive: 0`; the rest of the app only sees `TextModel` and `RoleplayModel`. Watch: structured output varies by provider and model, and every reply depends on it; Thinking is exposed differently by each; there's nothing to unload for a cloud model; and a cloud service applies its own content rules, so a dark Roleplay the local heretic plays may be refused. About a day.
    4. **ComfyUI as an image backend** (below): about 2–3 days, plus a workflow per model.
    5. **Voices in the browser**, as an option on the Voice tab: the Web Speech API (no install, the system's voices, only pitch and rate) or a small model through transformers.js on WebGPU (Kokoro, 82M, preset voices; size and quality to check). Neither designs a voice from a description or clones one, as the voice service does, so a Character would pick a preset (gap 4 of the audit below). The web app would speak the line itself, with nothing on the server.
  - *3D on Windows:* **SHARP** yes (Apple's code is PyTorch and runs on CUDA; `scene/make.py` hard-codes `mps`). **TripoSplat** yes (VAST built it for CUDA; our vendored copy was moved to `mps`). **LiTo** not as run here (mlx-spatial is an MLX port); on Windows it would need Apple's original LiTo code, and whether Apple published runnable inference code for it hasn't been checked.

- **ComfyUI as a backend, for Windows with an NVIDIA card.** mflux is Apple Silicon only, so the app as built renders only on a Mac. ComfyUI runs on both, and has a local HTTP and WebSocket API (checked 2026-10-02 against the installed ComfyUI 0.37.4, Comfy Desktop, on `127.0.0.1:8188`):
  - `POST /prompt` queues a workflow (its "Export (API)" JSON) and returns a `prompt_id`;
  - `GET /history/{prompt_id}` or `/api/jobs/{id}` lists its outputs, and `GET /view?filename=…&type=output` fetches one;
  - `POST /upload/image` sends an input picture (for Upscale, edits, a panorama, TripoSplat);
  - the `/ws?clientId=…` WebSocket reports which node is running and step progress;
  - `POST /interrupt` and `/api/jobs/{id}/cancel` cancel, and `POST /free` unloads its models (useful before a voice or a scene, as for the Text Model);
  - `GET /object_info` lists installed nodes, and `/system_stats` GPU memory.

  It would slot in behind `ImageGenerator` as a third generator beside mflux and the placeholder: one workflow template per Image Model (and the upscaler), with the prompt, seed, steps and size filled in, chosen in Settings. Unlike mflux's process per image (ADR 0004), ComfyUI is a long-running server that keeps models loaded: faster renders, but it holds the memory until `/free`. What wouldn't come along on Windows: the voice service (mlx-audio is MLX, Mac only: Higgs TTS 3 and Qwen3-TTS have PyTorch releases that run on CUDA, or a ComfyUI TTS node), SHARP's script (hardcoded to `mps`; it runs on CUDA too, by changing the device), and the saved quantized copies (mflux's format; ComfyUI has its own fp8 and GGUF models). Ollama runs on Windows as is.
- **Viggle's 9-step mode for Qwen-Image 2.1.** Settings → Fast uses Viggle's turbo LoRA at 6 steps (v0.3, `docs/models.md`). Viggle's v0.3 also has a 9-step mode, with the same LoRA: 7 turbo steps, then the LoRA is switched off and the base model takes the last two (its own sigmas, `[1.0, 0.9583, 0.9167, 0.875, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25, 1/6, 1/12]`; the prompt's cached K/V are computed again on the first base step). Finer detail and small text right more often, at ~1.4–1.5× the time of 6 steps; Viggle says it works in diffusers and its Space only. mflux 0.21's `viggle_turbo` scheduler requires exactly 6 steps and can't switch a LoRA off partway (checked 2026-10-04). Doing it ourselves means a Python script on mflux's internals (a custom scheduler, a hook after step 7), which any mflux update could break. Trigger: mflux supports it, or 6 steps looks too soft.
- **A figure inside its 2.5D scene: SHARP's background with a TripoSplat or LiTo figure.** Tried 2026-10-04 (docs/research/image-to-3d.md, "a figure inside its 2.5D scene"): works, and makes a figure look far better than alone on black, though the person becomes the figure's redrawing at the picture's own view. Method: remove the splats SHARP put inside the person's cut-out mask (and not far behind it), turn the figure to face the camera, scale and move it to cover the mask from the picture's camera (outline fit 0.90–0.95), append it in SHARP's axes. To build into the app: when a Frame has a scene and a figure, view them together (a "with the room" choice on the figure's viewer, or a combined file made after both), orbiting as the figure viewer does but with the room only holding to ~30°; and decide what the picture's view should show (the figure, or SHARP's person until it turns).
- **Deno or Node.** Kept on Deno for now (checked 2026-10-02). TypeScript isn't the reason: Deno runs it natively and Node 24 runs it too, by stripping the types. Moving to Node would touch ~100 uses of Deno APIs (file I/O, `Deno.serve`, `Deno.Command`, `Deno.env`, signals), 207 `Deno.test` tests, the `@std/*` imports (path, assert, yaml, front-matter), `URLPattern` routing and the tasks: a day or two of mechanical work for little gain. Deno gives no build step, built-in fmt/lint/test and a lockfile; Node is already used, only for `vue-tsc`. Porting to Go or WASM later is eased not by translating the types but by the shape: each service behind a small interface with plain data in and out (below), which carries over to any language.
- **AI services behind interfaces: five gaps** (audited 2026-10-02, with Windows and other providers in mind). The web app is cleanly separate: it imports nothing from the server and no Deno APIs, and talks to it only through `web/src/api.ts` (three `fetch` call sites, JSON and SSE) at `/api/…`, which Vite proxies to :8787, so any backend serving the same routes works. On the server, every service is an interface chosen once in `main.ts` (`AppDeps`): `TextModel` and `RoleplayModel` (Ollama), `ImageGenerator` (mflux, or SVG placeholders), `VoiceEngine` (the mlx-audio service), `SceneMaker` (SHARP), `FigureMaker` (TripoSplat, LiTo), and the stores (`SessionStore`, `SettingsStore`, `ScenarioLibrary`, `QuantizedStore`). Swapping a TTS means a new `VoiceEngine` and one line in `main.ts`. The gaps:
  1. *The image model list is mflux's and used everywhere.* `imageModels.ts` holds mflux commands and model ids; Settings validation (`settings.ts`), the `/api/settings/options` menu, steps, Quantize (mflux's saved 8-bit copies) and the Upscaler (SeedVR2) all read it. A ComfyUI or other backend would bring its own list: the `ImageGenerator` should advertise its models, sizes and options, and Settings validate against that.
  2. *The chat message type is Ollama's.* `ChatMessage` lives in `ollamaChat.ts`, and `roleplay/prompt.ts`, `art.ts`, `voice.ts`, `suggest.ts` and `testing.ts` import it from there. Move it to a neutral module.
  3. *`app.ts` defaults to Ollama itself:* `roleplayModel` falls back to `ollamaRoleplayModel` inside `createHandler` instead of being injected from `main.ts` like the rest, and `TextModelInfo` comes from `ollama.ts`.
  4. *Voices assume a cloning TTS.* `VoiceEngine` is `design` (a voice from a description) plus `speak` (cloning the designed reference clip), with pace, sound and whisper; a provider without cloning (preset voices only) would need another way to keep a Character's voice consistent, e.g. picking a preset once per Character.
  5. *The Python scripts are hard-coded to the Mac GPU (`mps`):* `scene/make.py` (SHARP), `figure/make.py` (TripoSplat) and `figure/lito.py`'s cut-out. They'd take a device (or detect CUDA) on Windows; LiTo runs only on MLX (Apple Silicon) at all.
